What rising rates mean for Collin County buyers

Jul 18, 2026 · 4 min read

Mortgage rates are well above where they sat during the 2020–2021 pandemic-era lows, and for buyers shopping in Collin County that mostly shows up as a smaller comfortable price range and more competition for well-priced homes under that new ceiling — not a market that's stopped moving. You can check today's actual 30- and 15-year rate on the ledger at the top of the homepage, which updates automatically each weekday.

How much does a rate increase actually change my budget?

Even a small move in rate changes your monthly payment more than most buyers expect, since it compounds over a 30-year loan — a single percentage point can shift what you can comfortably afford by tens of thousands of dollars. The practical effect is usually a lower comfortable purchase price for the same monthly payment you had in mind, which is why getting a real, current pre-approval matters more in a moving-rate environment than it did when rates were flat.

Is it still worth buying in Collin County right now?

For most buyers, yes — waiting on rates to drop further usually means competing with more buyers once they do, which tends to push prices back up and erase whatever you saved on rate. The better question isn't "should I wait for a better rate," it's "does this specific home, at today's numbers, make sense for how long I plan to stay." That answer is different for every buyer, which is exactly the kind of thing worth actually talking through rather than guessing at.

What loan options can help offset a higher rate?

A temporary rate buydown — where the seller or builder pays to lower your rate for the first year or two of the loan — can meaningfully soften the first-year payment shock, and it's becoming more common as a negotiating point in Collin County right now. An adjustable-rate mortgage (ARM) is another option worth understanding, especially if you don't plan to stay in the home for the full 30 years, though it comes with its own tradeoffs once the fixed period ends. Neither is automatically the right move — it depends on your specific timeline and risk tolerance.

What should Collin County buyers do differently in this rate environment?

A few things make a real difference right now: get pre-approved with more than one lender so you can compare actual offers (rate quotes vary more between lenders than people expect), ask about temporary rate buy-downs on new construction and seller-financed deals, and lock your rate once you're under contract rather than floating it and hoping. None of these are complicated, but they're easy to skip if nobody walks you through them.
