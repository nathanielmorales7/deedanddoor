What rising rates mean for Collin County buyers

Jul 18, 2026 · 4 min read

Mortgage rates have moved higher this year, and for buyers shopping in Collin County that mostly shows up as a smaller comfortable price range and more competition for well-priced homes under that new ceiling — not a market that's stopped moving.

How much does a rate increase actually change my budget?

Even a small move in rate changes your monthly payment more than most buyers expect, since it compounds over a 30-year loan. The practical effect is usually a lower comfortable purchase price for the same monthly payment you had in mind — which is why getting a real, current pre-approval matters more in a moving-rate environment than it did when rates were flat.

Is it still worth buying in Collin County right now?

For most buyers, yes — waiting on rates to drop further usually means competing with more buyers once they do, which tends to push prices back up. The better question isn't "should I wait for a better rate," it's "does this specific home, at today's numbers, make sense for how long I plan to stay." That answer is different for every buyer, which is exactly the kind of thing worth actually talking through rather than guessing at.

What should Collin County buyers do differently in this rate environment?

A few things make a real difference right now: get pre-approved with more than one lender so you can compare actual offers, ask about temporary rate buy-downs (sometimes the seller will pay for these), and lock your rate once you're under contract rather than floating it and hoping. None of these are complicated, but they're easy to skip if nobody walks you through them.
