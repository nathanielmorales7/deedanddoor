Why Flower Mound

A deliberately low-density town between Dallas and Fort Worth

Flower Mound has long used its own zoning policy to limit high-density development, which has kept it an affluent, larger-lot alternative to its denser neighbors while still sitting roughly equidistant — about 20 miles — from both Dallas and Fort Worth. It's zoned primarily to Lewisville ISD, and its proximity to Grapevine Lake adds another layer of appeal.

Housing here tends toward larger, more established lots than newer master-planned suburbs nearby, with a mix of mature neighborhoods and newer development on the town's edges, consistently putting it near the top of best-places-to-live rankings in DFW.

FAQ
What school district serves Flower Mound?+
Most of the town is zoned to Lewisville ISD. I always confirm the exact zoning for a specific address before you commit to a house, since a few edge areas can differ.
Why are lots bigger in Flower Mound than nearby suburbs?+
The town has long used its own zoning policy to limit high-density development, which has kept lot sizes larger and density lower than many comparable DFW suburbs — part of why it's consistently ranked among the best places to live in the metroplex.
How do Flower Mound's neighborhoods differ from each other?+
Mostly by home age and proximity to Grapevine Lake — some of the most established, larger-lot neighborhoods sit near the lake, while newer development is concentrated on the town's edges. Worth comparing a few before deciding.
What's the commute like from Flower Mound?+
Flower Mound sits roughly equidistant from Dallas and Fort Worth, about 20 miles from downtown Dallas and a similar distance from downtown Fort Worth. Commute times vary depending on which part of town you're in.
