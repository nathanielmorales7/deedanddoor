Why Richardson

A long-standing tech hub with established, walkable pockets

Richardson has anchored North Texas's tech and telecom industry for decades — long known as the "Telecom Corridor" — and more recently added CityLine, a major mixed-use development anchored by State Farm's regional campus. It's zoned primarily to Richardson ISD, one of the more consistently rated districts in the metroplex.

Housing is largely established, with mid-century neighborhoods throughout much of the city and newer, denser development concentrated around CityLine, giving buyers a mix of traditional suburban and more urban options within the same city.

FAQ
What school district serves Richardson?+
Most of the city is zoned to Richardson ISD. I always confirm the exact zoning for a specific address before you commit to a house, since a few edge areas can differ.
What's it like living near CityLine?+
Walkable and newer, with apartments and condos close to major employers — a different feel than Richardson's more traditional single-family neighborhoods elsewhere in the city.
How do Richardson's neighborhoods differ from each other?+
Mostly by home age and density — established mid-century neighborhoods make up much of the city, while CityLine offers newer, denser options. Worth comparing a few before deciding.
What's the commute like from Richardson?+
Richardson sits along US-75, roughly 15 miles from downtown Dallas. Commute times vary depending on which part of the city you're in and which direction you're headed.
