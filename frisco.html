Why Frisco

One of DFW's fastest-growing cities

Frisco has been one of the fastest-growing cities in the country for over a decade — corporate relocations, new retail, and a steady pipeline of new-construction neighborhoods have reshaped the city. It's zoned primarily to Frisco ISD, with easy access to the Dallas North Tollway and about 30 miles to downtown Dallas.

The city covers a wide range of home styles, from established neighborhoods with mature trees to new construction on the city's edges. Lot sizes, HOA structures, and amenities vary more from one community to the next than they might look from the street, so comparing a few side by side is worth the time before deciding.

Areas to know

Frisco isn't one market — it's several

A few of the neighborhoods and areas buyers ask about most:

Phillips Creek Ranch

Master-planned community with a resort-style amenity center, popular with families wanting newer construction.

Starwood

Established, gated, golf-course community — one of Frisco's more prestigious and mature neighborhoods.

The Trails

Centrally located with easy access to the Dallas North Tollway, a mix of home ages and styles.

Panther Creek Estates

Larger lots and custom-built homes for buyers wanting more space and fewer cookie-cutter floor plans.

Frisco Square / Downtown

Walkable, mixed-use area near City Hall with condos, townhomes, and easy access to dining and events.

Near The Star / PGA Frisco

Proximity to the Dallas Cowboys headquarters and the PGA of America campus continues to drive demand on this side of the city.

FAQ
What school district serves Frisco?+
Most of Frisco is zoned to Frisco ISD, though a handful of neighborhoods on the city's edges fall into neighboring districts like Little Elm ISD or Prosper ISD. I can confirm the exact zoning for any specific address before you fall in love with a house.
Should I buy new construction or resale in Frisco?+
Both have a place here. New construction often comes with builder incentives like rate buydowns or closing-cost credits, but less room to negotiate on the price itself. Resale can offer more flexibility and often a more established neighborhood, but may need updating. Happy to walk through the tradeoffs for your specific situation.
What's the commute like from Frisco?+
Frisco sits along the Dallas North Tollway, roughly 30 miles from downtown Dallas. Commute times vary a lot depending on which part of the city you're in and which direction you're headed, so it's worth checking against your specific workplace.
How do Frisco's neighborhoods differ from each other?+
Quite a bit — lot sizes, HOA structures, amenities, and even school zoning can vary from one community to the next, even within a few miles. Worth comparing a few before deciding, and I'm glad to put together a side-by-side for your criteria.
