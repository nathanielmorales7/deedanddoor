Why McKinney

Historic core, modern growth

McKinney has one of the most distinct identities in DFW: a genuinely historic, walkable downtown anchoring a city that's also added thousands of new homes over the last decade. It's zoned primarily to McKinney ISD, with easy access to US-75 and about 32 miles to downtown Dallas.

The city spans a wide range of home ages and styles — from century-old homes near the downtown square to new construction on the city's edges — so matching the right area to what you're looking for matters more here than in cities with a more uniform housing stock.

Areas to know

McKinney's range, from historic to brand new

A few of the areas buyers ask about most:

Historic Downtown McKinney

Walkable square with shops and restaurants, surrounded by older homes with character.

Stonebridge Ranch

One of the city's largest and most established master-planned communities, with multiple amenity centers and a private beach club.

Trinity Falls

Newer master-planned community on the city's north side built around trails, parks, and a resort-style amenity center.

Craig Ranch

Golf-course community straddling McKinney and its border with other suburbs, with a strong mix of upscale retail nearby.

Tucker Hill

New-urbanist, walkable community near downtown with a mix of home styles and front-porch streetscapes.

Erwin Farms

Established neighborhood convenient to US-75, known for mature trees and larger lots compared to newer builds.

FAQ
What school district serves McKinney?+
Most of the city is zoned to McKinney ISD, though some neighborhoods on the edges fall into Prosper ISD or Melissa ISD. Zoning can vary street to street in a few areas, so I always confirm it for a specific address before you commit.
What's the commute like from McKinney?+
McKinney sits along US-75, roughly 32 miles from downtown Dallas. Commute times vary significantly by which part of the city you're in — the north side can be a noticeably longer drive than areas closer to 75.
What's it like living near Historic Downtown McKinney?+
Homes near the square tend to be older and often need updating, but you get walkable access to shops, restaurants, and a genuinely historic streetscape that's hard to find elsewhere in the metroplex.
How is McKinney different from Frisco or Prosper?+
McKinney generally spans a wider range of home ages and styles in one city — from historic homes downtown to new construction on the edges — while Frisco and Prosper lean more toward newer, master-planned development throughout.
