DFW inventory is finally loosening up

Aug 22, 2026 · 4 min read

Active listings across North Texas have been climbing through the second half of summer, and for buyers who spent the last couple of years fighting over anything decent that hit the market, that shift is worth paying attention to. More inventory generally means more time to think, more room to negotiate, and fewer bidding wars — though how much it changes things depends a lot on price point and submarket.

What does rising inventory actually mean for buyers right now?

More active listings means more choices and less pressure to make a same-day decision — buyers typically get more time to think, more leverage to ask for repairs or closing-cost help, and fewer multiple-offer situations on well-priced homes. It doesn't mean homes are sitting unsold or that sellers are desperate, just that the balance of power is shifting back toward a more normal, less frantic pace.

The change shows up first in the entry-level and move-up price bands, where competition has been fiercest the last few years. Luxury and highly specific inventory — a certain school zone, a certain lot size — can still move fast even while the broader market loosens.

Why does DFW inventory tend to loosen up heading into fall?

DFW inventory almost always builds through summer and into early fall as spring listings that haven't sold yet stay on the market and new sellers keep testing the waters, then tightens back up once the holidays slow both new listings and buyer traffic. This is a normal seasonal pattern, not a signal on its own that something's wrong with the market.

What matters more than the season is the trend line — whether inventory is building faster or slower than it typically does this time of year, and how quickly the homes that do sell are actually moving.

Does more inventory automatically mean lower prices?

Not directly — inventory and price are related but not the same thing, and a market can see inventory rise for months before prices actually soften, especially if overall demand stays steady. What more inventory does more reliably is slow down appreciation and give buyers more negotiating room on price, credits, and terms, even before list prices themselves start moving.

Watch days-on-market and the sale-to-list price ratio, not just the number of listings — those two numbers together tell you far more about real negotiating leverage than inventory count alone.

How should buyers adjust their strategy when inventory rises?

A looser market rewards patience and preparation more than speed. A few adjustments worth making:

  1. Get pre-approved before you start touring, not after you find something — sellers still favor a clean, ready offer even when they have fewer competing bids.
  2. Ask for what you actually want — repair credits, closing cost help, a longer option period — rather than defaulting to a bare-bones offer out of old bidding-war habits.
  3. Widen your search a little — with more on the market, it's worth looking at homes that would have been snapped up before you could tour them a year ago.
  4. Don't assume every seller knows the market has shifted — some list prices still reflect last year's pace, which is exactly where the negotiating room shows up.

What should sellers do differently in a looser market?

Pricing accurately from day one matters more, not less, once inventory rises — overpriced listings sit, collect price-drop history, and end up selling for less than if they'd been priced right from the start. Presentation — photos, staging, timely showing access — also carries more weight when buyers have real alternatives to compare against.

Where can I track DFW inventory numbers myself?

NTREIS, the MLS covering most of North Texas, is the most authoritative source local agents pull from, and regional housing data from FRED (the St. Louis Fed) is free and public if you want to track broader trend lines yourself. If you'd rather skip the spreadsheet, that's exactly the kind of read I can walk you through for whatever specific area or price point you're watching.
