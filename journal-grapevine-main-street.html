A Saturday on Grapevine's Historic Main Street

Oct 23, 2026 · 3 min read
Photo by Wildstoo · Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

What makes Grapevine's Main Street worth a Saturday?

Grapevine's Historic Main Street is a revitalized downtown that mixes wine tasting rooms, local shops, and restaurants into a few walkable blocks. The city rebuilt the district in the 1990s around its original early-1900s buildings, and it's stayed a genuine small-town square rather than turning into another strip of chain storefronts.

Where should a Saturday start?

Grapevine Main Station is the easiest starting point — a former train depot now home to Harvest Hall, a food hall with more than a dozen vendors under one roof and a large outdoor patio. A 150-foot Observation Tower next door gives a view over the whole district before heading out on foot.

What's the Urban Wine Trail?

The Urban Wine Trail is a self-guided list of downtown tasting rooms the city's visitors bureau put together — covering spots like Messina Hof Grapevine, Cave Society, Weinstein's Tasting Room, and Grape Vine Springs Winery. Hitting every stop in one afternoon takes a few hours and a food break in between, so most people pick three or four rather than the whole list.

Where's good to eat, and where's dessert?

Tolbert's serves chili from the family that helped start the Terlingua Chili Cookoff, about as Texas a lunch as Main Street gets. For dessert, Beth Marie's Old Fashioned Ice Cream is a longtime local favorite known for flavors that don't show up anywhere else.

What else is worth knowing before you go?

The Grapevine Vintage Railroad runs tours on a restored 1920s-era train, with themed rides through the year. Main Street also runs ghost tours on Friday and Saturday nights that start with a glass of wine, and Vetro Glassblowers offers hands-on glassblowing classes by reservation for something different than another tasting room.

When's the best time to visit?

Main Street runs festivals through most of the year: a spring Wine Trail and blessing of the vines each April, Main Street Fest each May with live music and a carnival, and GrapeFest each September, billed as the largest wine festival in the Southwest. Grapevine also leans hard into the holidays every winter, with a reputation around DFW as something close to the region's Christmas headquarters.
