DFW inventory is finally loosening up

Aug 22, 2026 · 4 min read

If you tried to buy in Dallas–Fort Worth a couple of years ago, you probably remember the drill: a new listing would go up on a Thursday, showings would be packed all weekend, and an offer above asking with no contingencies was often what it took just to be competitive. That market hasn't been the reality here for a while now, and this summer made the shift harder to ignore — inventory across the region has been building steadily, and buyers are finally getting some room to breathe.

What's actually changed in the numbers?

Active resale listings across DFW climbed noticeably through the spring and summer, and months of supply — a measure of how long it would take to sell through everything currently on the market at the current sales pace — has pushed up into the range generally considered balanced-to-buyer-friendly for resale homes, a level this market hasn't sat at in years. New construction has followed a similar pattern, with builders carrying more standing inventory than they were pricing to move a couple of years back.

None of this happened overnight. It's been a gradual build through 2026 as more sellers listed, more new construction hit the market, and buyer demand cooled against higher borrowing costs — not a single dramatic event, but a steady shift in the balance of power between buyers and sellers.

Does more inventory mean prices are dropping?

Not really — at least not in the way "more inventory" sometimes gets portrayed. Homes that are priced right and show well are still selling close to list price; sellers in most DFW submarkets are still capturing the large majority of their original asking price when the home is priced correctly from day one. What's changed isn't so much the price sellers get on a well-positioned home — it's how long it takes to get there, and how much negotiating happens along the way on homes that aren't priced or presented well.

What does this actually mean if you're buying right now?

More selection, more time to think, and more leverage than you've had in years. You can typically see a home more than once before writing an offer, request inspection repairs or a credit instead of waiving inspection entirely, and negotiate on closing costs or rate buydowns — especially on listings that have been sitting for a few weeks. The tradeoff is that a genuinely well-priced, well-located home in a sought-after school zone can still draw competition, so "the market has loosened up" doesn't mean every home is a slow, easy negotiation. It means you're no longer the one apologizing for wanting to think it over for a day.

Should sellers be worried?

Not worried, but the game has changed. Two years ago, an overpriced or under-prepped listing could still sell fast simply because there was so little else on the market to compare it to. That cushion is gone. Homes that are priced to current comps, show well in photos and in person, and are marketed properly are still moving and still closing near list. Homes that are priced on what the neighbor got in 2023, or that skip basic prep, are the ones sitting — and every extra week on market tends to invite lower offers, not higher ones. Pricing and presentation matter more now than they have in a long time.
