Frisco just ranked the #1 real estate market in America

Sep 25, 2026 · 5 min read

WalletHub's annual study of the 300 best real estate markets in the country just named Frisco the #1 market in America — and McKinney landed right behind it at #2. Denton came in at #5 and Allen at #8, which means four Collin and Denton County cities made the national top 8. This isn't a "feels like it's booming" kind of story — it's backed by hard numbers on affordability, job growth, and housing supply, and it's worth understanding both for what it says about the market and where the opportunity actually is.

What did WalletHub's 2026 ranking actually measure?

WalletHub scored all 300 cities across categories like affordability, economic environment, and quality of local real estate market, then ranked them nationally. Frisco topped the "affordability and economic environment" category outright — a notable result for a city that isn't exactly known for being cheap, which says more about how much income and job growth have kept pace with home prices there than about low prices themselves.

Why did Frisco rank #1 nationally?

Three numbers drove the result: nearly 47% of Frisco's housing stock was built between 2010 and 2024, the highest share of newer homes in the entire study, the city posted the 7th-best job growth rate in the country, and its home prices consume a smaller share of household income than 95% of the other cities ranked. Put simply, Frisco has been building enough new supply and creating enough jobs to keep housing costs from outrunning what people actually earn there — which is rare in a market that's grown this fast.

How did McKinney, Denton, and Allen do?

McKinney took the #2 spot nationally, right behind Frisco, with Denton at #5 and Allen at #8 — all four landing in the top 8 of 300 cities studied. For comparison, Dallas came in at #71 overall and Plano at #61. The pattern is clear: it's specifically the fast-growing Collin and Denton County suburbs, not the core city, that are driving DFW's national reputation right now.

What's actually fueling this growth?

Rankings like this don't happen in a vacuum — they track real hiring. Public Storage is relocating its entire corporate headquarters to Frisco's HALL Park this year, bringing roughly 300 jobs and 119,000 square feet of office space, the largest out-of-state HQ relocation to DFW by square footage since 2021. A few miles south, AT&T is building a new 54-acre global headquarters campus in Plano's Legacy District, consolidating offices from across the Metroplex into one location. Jobs like these are exactly the kind of economic activity that shows up in a ranking like WalletHub's.

What does this mean if you're buying in Frisco or McKinney right now?

A national #1 ranking tends to draw more attention and more buyers, which can tighten competition on well-priced homes in the areas driving the result. A few things worth doing because of it:

  1. Move on pre-approval now rather than after you start touring — rankings like this can shift buyer sentiment fast, even before the numbers on the ground actually change.
  2. Look at newer-build inventory closely — Frisco's high share of 2010-2024 construction means there's real selection in newer homes, not just resale.
  3. Factor in the job growth, not just the ranking — new employers moving in is a better long-term signal for home values than a single year's award.

What does this mean if you're selling in Frisco or McKinney right now?

National attention is free marketing, but it doesn't replace accurate pricing — buyers moving here because of a ranking are still going to compare your home against current local comps, not against the headline. It's a good moment to make sure your listing tells the same story the ranking does: a well-maintained home in a market backed by real job growth, not just a hot label.

Where can I see the full ranking myself?

WalletHub publishes its full 300-city methodology and rankings directly on its site if you want to see exactly how every DFW-area city scored.
