Inside McKinney's historic downtown district

Aug 15, 2026 · 5 min read

McKinney is one of the fastest-growing cities in the country, but its historic downtown square is the one part of town that still feels like it did decades ago — brick storefronts, a working courthouse, and a walkable grid of streets that's become one of the more distinctive pockets to live in anywhere in Collin County.

What is downtown McKinney actually known for?

Downtown McKinney centers on its historic town square — a National Register Historic District anchored by the 1876 Collin County Courthouse, locally owned restaurants and shops, and a regular calendar of festivals and farmers markets that draw people in from across North Texas, not just the immediate neighborhood.

It's the kind of downtown a lot of newer DFW suburbs are still trying to build from scratch. McKinney's has simply been there the whole time, which is part of its appeal.

What kind of homes will I find near the square?

Homes within walking distance of the square are a mix of early-1900s bungalows and Craftsman-style houses on smaller, walkable lots, alongside newer infill construction built to match the historic character of the area. Further out, downtown gives way to more typical DFW suburban subdivisions with larger lots and newer builds.

Because many of the historic homes sit within the district's protected zone, exterior renovations can be subject to design review — worth understanding early if a fixer-upper near the square is on your list.

What's the school situation for families moving downtown?

Downtown McKinney falls within McKinney Independent School District, one of the larger districts in Collin County, though which specific campus a given address feeds into can vary block by block near the district's boundary lines. I don't put stock in national ranking sites for this — a home's actual campus feed, current ratings, and boundary lines are worth confirming directly for any specific address before deciding a home is or isn't the right fit.

How's the commute from downtown McKinney into Dallas?

Downtown McKinney sits roughly 30 to 35 minutes from downtown Dallas without traffic, mostly via US-75, with that number stretching meaningfully during peak commute hours like most of North Texas. A regional train station in downtown McKinney adds a transit option some commuters use to avoid driving the corridor directly.

Who tends to love living in downtown McKinney?

Buyers drawn to downtown McKinney are usually looking for walkability and character that most DFW suburbs can't offer — an actual town center to walk to, older homes with real architectural detail, and a slower, more established feel than a brand-new subdivision. It tends to appeal less to buyers who want a larger new-construction floor plan or a big backyard, both easier to find outside the historic core.

Is downtown McKinney a good long-term investment?

Established, walkable historic districts in growing metros have generally held value well over time, because the supply of homes that close to a real town center is fixed — nobody's building more 1910s bungalows two blocks from the square. That scarcity is the core argument for downtown McKinney as a long-term hold, though as with any specific address, the right comparison is always current comps, not a general trend.
