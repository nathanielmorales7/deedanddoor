Buying a home in DFW, step by step

Buying a home is one of those things nobody does often enough to get good at — most people do it two or three times in their life, which means every single time it feels brand new. Here's the process laid out plainly, plus honest answers to the questions I get asked most.

The process

What actually happens, in order

Get pre-approved before you fall in love with anything

A pre-approval letter from a lender tells you your real budget and tells sellers you're a serious buyer. Skipping this step is how people end up touring homes $150,000 outside what they can actually finance.

Define your must-haves and target neighborhoods

School zone, commute, yard size, single story versus two — the more specific you are up front, the less time gets wasted touring homes that were never going to work.

Tour homes and compare against the list

Photos lie a little (or a lot). Touring in person is where you find out what a listing didn't mention — the busy street, the low water pressure, the neighbor's trampoline empire.

Make a competitive, well-structured offer

Price is only one part of an offer. Option period length, option fee, earnest money, and closing timeline all shape how a seller reads your offer against competing ones.

Option period, inspection, and negotiating repairs

Once under contract, your inspection happens during the option period. This is your window to renegotiate price or repairs, or walk away outright, before your earnest money is meaningfully at risk.

Appraisal, final walkthrough, and closing day

Your lender orders an appraisal to confirm the home is worth what you're paying. A final walkthrough right before closing confirms nothing's changed, then you sign, fund, and get keys.

Buyer FAQ

Questions I hear on nearly every deal

Your lender's pre-approval number is your ceiling, not your target. A more useful number is your comfortable monthly payment — principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and any HOA dues combined — worked backward into a price range. Getting pre-approved early is what turns "afford" from a guess into a real number.

Once I know your must-haves, I set up a saved search that alerts you the moment something matching hits the MLS — often before it shows up on the big consumer sites. We tour what's promising, compare notes after each one, and refine the list as your preferences sharpen (they always do).

In a balanced or buyer-leaning market, price matters less than people assume — a clean offer with a reasonable timeline, a solid pre-approval, and a fair (not excessive) option period often beats a slightly higher offer that looks shakier. In a competitive multiple-offer situation, a shorter option period and stronger earnest money can matter as much as price.

You pay the seller a small option fee for the unrestricted right to terminate the contract for any reason during a set window, typically 7-10 days. This is when your inspection happens — if something serious turns up, we can renegotiate price or repairs, or walk away with your earnest money intact.

Budget roughly 2-4% of the purchase price for buyer-side closing costs: loan origination and lender fees, an appraisal, a survey if one's needed, prepaid taxes and insurance, and title-related fees. Your lender provides a Loan Estimate early on that breaks this down so there are no surprises at the closing table.
