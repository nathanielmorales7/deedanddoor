A Saturday in Southlake Town Square

Nov 8, 2026 · 3 min read
Photo by NETarrantMan · Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

What makes Southlake Town Square worth a Saturday?

Cooper & Stebbins laid this district out in the late 1990s on a Texas courthouse-square street grid — Phase I opened in 1999, Phase II in 2006. The result is a 130-acre area built to park once and walk the rest of, with Town Hall and the public library anchoring the center, Rustin Park's gazebo and pond nearby, and more than 100 stores and 27 restaurants filling the blocks around them.

What's new at the square this year?

A steady run of national openings. Crate & Barrel is opening a nearly 19,000-square-foot store in 2026, alongside On Running, Reformation, and Ascension Coffee — Rothy's has already opened its doors. Trader Joe's has been a fixture for years, and the square keeps landing the kind of retail openings that usually go to bigger cities first.

What does a typical Saturday there look like?

Coffee first, then a walk, with on-street parking and sidewalks right up against the storefronts. Lunch covers Tex-Mex, Italian, steakhouse, and fast-casual within a few blocks of each other. The afternoon is for browsing — home goods, boutique fashion, a bookstore — all still within that same walk.

What's happening this particular Saturday?

The holidays kick off on November 21. Southlake's "Home for the Holidays" runs from 4:30 to 8:30 p.m. right on the square, with the 30-foot tree lighting at 6 p.m. sharp followed by a fireworks show. Kids get a three-lane snow hill and photos with Santa, and local school groups perform on stage through the evening. It's free, parking is free, and it's been a Southlake tradition since 1999.

Why do families specifically target Southlake?

The schools, not just the shopping. Carroll ISD carries an A+ rating from Niche and ranks third out of 874 school districts statewide — a big part of why families house-hunt here specifically, not just the walkability of the downtown.
