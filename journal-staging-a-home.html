Staging a home in under a week

Jul 11, 2026 · 4 min read

You don't need a full renovation to stage a home well. A week is enough time to declutter, deep clean, fix the small visible flaws, and set up a few key rooms to photograph well — and that's what actually moves the needle with buyers, not a remodel.

What should I prioritize if I only have a few days?

Start with the three things that show up in every listing photo and every walkthrough: clutter, cleanliness, and light. Clear counters, closets, and floors of anything that isn't earning its place in the photo. A genuinely deep clean — not a tidy-up — makes a bigger difference than most sellers expect. And open every blind and curtain; natural light is one of the cheapest upgrades a listing can get.

Which rooms matter most to stage?

Buyers spend the most attention on the living room, the primary bedroom, and the kitchen — so if your week is tight, that's where the effort goes first. Neutral bedding, a cleared kitchen counter, and furniture arranged to make the room feel larger rather than fuller all do more work than people assume before their first showing.

Should I hire a professional stager, or do it myself?

A professional stager makes the most sense for a vacant home, where empty rooms tend to photograph small and cold and buyers have a hard time picturing furniture placement on their own. For an occupied home that's already furnished reasonably well, a lighter touch — decluttering, depersonalizing, and rearranging what you already own — often gets you most of the benefit without the cost of a full staging package. Either way, the photographer matters as much as the staging itself; even a beautifully staged room falls flat in bad photos.

What's not worth the money right before listing?

Skip anything that qualifies as a real renovation — new flooring, a kitchen remodel, major landscaping — this close to listing. Those projects rarely return their full cost before closing, and the timeline usually doesn't work anyway. If you're weighing a bigger project, it's worth a quick conversation about which repairs actually affect your sale price before you spend on any of them.

What should I do with pets and personal items before showings?

Buyers picture themselves living in a home, and it's harder to do that surrounded by someone else's family photos, collections, or pet gear. Pack away personal photos and most decor, keep pet bowls, beds, and litter boxes out of sight during showings, and if possible, have pets somewhere else during the showing itself — not every buyer is comfortable with an unfamiliar dog greeting them at the door, however friendly.
